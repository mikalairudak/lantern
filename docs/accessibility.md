# Accessibility

Lantern aims at WCAG 2.2 Level AA. This document says what holds today, what holds it, what is known not to hold, and what only a person can check. It claims no conformance: some criteria are at risk, and everything it says about what assistive technology meets has been read from Chromium's accessibility tree and from logs of the page's status regions, never heard through a screen reader.

## Target and scope

- **Target:** WCAG 2.2 at levels A and AA. Patterns follow the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices, with native HTML first.
- **Pages:** what a visitor reaches. The start screen (`/`); a day (`/day/<city>` for a written day, `/day/<id>` for a kept one) in its plan and its chart, while it is written and once whole, with a changed word's proposal beside it, and every ending without a day; `/privacy`; the page for an address that names no day; the page for a failure. `/lab` and `/studio`, behind the owner's password, are out of scope.
- **Conditions:** both themes, a phone's width and a laptop's, 320 CSS px, zoom and text size, text spacing, reduced motion, forced colours.

## How it is checked

1. **The axe gate,** in every `pnpm e2e` and in CI ("The automated gate", below).
2. **Specs and unit tests that hold one behaviour each:** where the focus goes, a name, a status, a ring, a target. Each criterion below names its tests by file and title. An e2e test tagged `@frame` runs in every `pnpm e2e` on a laptop, and in CI only by hand (`docs/harness.md`, "End-to-end").
3. **Measurements in Chromium, not kept as tests:** keyboard walks forward and back, the accessibility tree, a log of every status region's text, contrast read from rendered pixels, hit-testing of every target, reflow at 320 px, zoom emulated by the viewport's size, text size by Chromium's default font size, WCAG's text spacing injected, and forced colours by Chromium's emulation. They run on the production build with live generation off; states that need live generation are reached by answering the routes in the browser with recorded responses. Chromium is the one engine measured, with WebKit for a few focus checks; Firefox, Safari, iOS and Android are not. "Measured in Chromium" below means this. A measurement holds for the code it measured, and a change to that code takes it again.
4. **A person, with assistive technology and the system's settings:** not yet ("Checks a person makes", below).

Each criterion's line starts with how it stands:

- **Holds:** nothing known fails it in what is checked.
- **Holds, with risks:** it holds as written, and an item under "Known issues" may fail it for some visitors.
- **At risk:** whether it holds turns on what a screen reader does or on a judgement; the items are under "Known issues".
- **Does not hold:** a known failure, under "Known issues".
- **Not applicable:** nothing on the site is what the criterion covers.

## The automated gate

`e2e/accessibility.spec.ts` runs axe over twelve states a visitor reaches, through the helper in `e2e/axe.ts` (`docs/decisions.md`, "axe checks the pages a visitor reaches against WCAG's A and AA rules, in the e2e").

- **Rules.** axe's rules tagged `wcag2a`, `wcag2aa`, `wcag21a`, `wcag21aa` and `wcag22aa`: WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 at A and AA. `target-size` (2.5.8), which axe leaves off by default, is turned on. axe's experimental rules do not run, `css-orientation-lock` among them. `axe-core` is pinned to one exact version in `package.json`, and the helper puts its script into the page itself, so no page policy can keep it out.
- **States.** The start screen; the start screen with a day of the visitor's being written; the start screen's answer to a text it cannot plan; a day's plan; a day's chart; a stop's details open on the chart; the energy word's list open; the day's menu open; a day being written, on its plan; a changed word's proposal beside the day; the privacy page; the page for an address that names no day.
- **Conditions.** Each state in the light and the dark theme, on the `desktop` project (Desktop Chrome) and the `phone` project (Pixel 7), under reduced motion: 24 tests a project, 48 in all. The dev server's own overlay, which production never serves, is left out of the scan.
- **What fails.** Any violation fails its state. A run that passed no rule fails, so a page where axe could not run never reads as clean. What axe reports as incomplete fails nothing; above all that is contrast over the plate paper, inside SVG and under layered elements, which it cannot compute.
- **When.** In every `pnpm e2e` on a laptop, and in CI's e2e shards on every pull request that changes more than inert paths and on every push to `main` (`docs/harness.md`, "Continuous integration").

**The one exemption.** On the energy word's list, axe reports `label-content-name-mismatch` for the options that carry a second line. Each option is named by its answer, the word shown first ("quiet"), and described by the line under it ("re-picks five stops · at once"). WCAG 2.5.3 asks that a control's name contain its visible label, and the name does, as its first word; axe counts every word inside the option as its label. The exemption names the rule and a selector that covers every option of that one list, in that one state, and when none of them is still reported the test fails, so it cannot outlive its cause. Whether voice control reaches an option by its first line is for a person to check.

**What axe cannot see.** It reads the markup at one moment. It does not hear what a screen reader says, nor whether a status is said once, twice or not at all (4.1.3). It does not follow the focus, so where the focus lands, whether it shows and whether something covers it are held by specs or not at all (2.4.3, 2.4.7, 2.4.11). It does not press keys (2.1.1, 2.1.2), raise content on hover (1.4.13), watch motion or time (2.2.2, 2.3.1), zoom, enlarge text or reflow the page (1.4.4, 1.4.10), or judge whether a name or a heading makes sense (2.4.6). It sees only its twelve states: the chart's hover names, the city list open, a proposal being written, the endings, a kept day's wait, the page for a failure and forced colours are not scanned. Every item under "Known issues" below passes the gate. A clean run is a floor, not a verdict.

## Criterion by criterion

Each line says how the criterion stands in the product as built, then what holds it. "axe:" names the gate's rules for the criterion, which run over its twelve states.

### Perceivable

- **1.1.1 Non-text Content (A).** Holds, with a risk. The index's engravings, the wordmark's lantern and every icon are hidden from assistive technology, and each control that shows one is named in words. The chart's drawing is hidden: each stop is a slider named by its time, with its energy and place as its value, and each other version is a button named by its place and the way it goes. The plan is the chart in text. A best-light sun is an image named "Best light: sunset". Risk: golden hour on the plan (Known issues). ## Checks a person makes

**No screen reader has been driven against Lantern, and no person has made these checks: none with a screen reader, voice control, the system's zoom or contrast themes.** Every statement above about what assistive technology meets rests on Chromium's accessibility tree, logs of the page's status regions, the tests named and measurements in Chromium. The checks below are for a person to make, each with what the product does as built; where an outcome is a known issue, it says so.

**Where to check.** A written day gives the same day each time: `/day/kanazawa` or `/day/porto`. A day this device has seen opens whole, so open it in a private window to watch it play in. A re-plan and the start screen's answers with live generation on need a preview deployment with it on, or a local run with the flag on, not the production site, where each live day spends the site's budget and each check counts as a visit.

### Screen readers

VoiceOver with Safari on macOS; NVDA with Firefox and with Chrome on Windows, in browse mode and in focus mode; VoiceOver with Safari on iOS; TalkBack with Chrome on Android. On each:

- **Landmarks and headings.** `/` has a `main`, a `contentinfo`, an h1 and an h2 over the index ("No city in mind? Start from one of these"); a day has one h1, the request as a sentence, and an h2 per stop in the plan. Every heading names its section, and the h1 is read with a space between each word.
- **Make a day.** Type "a calm day in Porto" and press Enter: the button is named for the wait ("Reading", then "Writing", or "Opening" for a day Lantern has written), a status says once what Lantern is doing, and the focus lands on the city word "Porto" as the day takes the page, its title announced. Nothing is said twice.
- **A city of a shared name.** With live generation on, type "Portland, a lively day" and press Enter; at "Which city?", Tab to one of the cities and press Enter. The focus lands on the button, named "Writing", and the status says the day is being written: note whether the line is said, cut or dropped as the focus lands with it. Then, in Safari on a Mac and on an iPhone, choose a city by a click or a tap: no focus ring is drawn on the button after a pointer's press (checked in Chromium alone).
- **An answer without a day.** On a local run with live generation off, type "Lisbon" and press Enter: "Lantern isn't writing new days right now. Start from one of the days below." is said once, without moving to it, and the focus stays on the button. Known at risk: going back to the start screen from a day while an answer still stands draws its status holding the answer again.
- **Read the plan.** Each stop reads its time, its name as a heading, what and where it is, until when, and its line; the sun and best-light words are read as words.
- **Swap a stop.** Press "Livelier: …" under a stop: the focus lands on the stop once it carries the version taken, so the stop is named by the place now standing as the focus lands, and a status says that place and what the headline now adds ("livelier at breakfast").
- **Change a word.** Open "calm": the list "The day's energy" opens on the current answer, each option reads its answer then what it would do ("re-picks five stops · at once"), and Escape returns to the word. A word Lantern assumed is described "assumed by Lantern". Pick another energy: a status says the level and what it re-picked.
- **The chart.** Press "Chart" in the group "View", then Tab into the chart. Each stop is a slider, "Energy at <time>", whose value says the energy and the place; the arrow keys change the version and the new place is said; Home and End reach the quietest and the liveliest.
- **A stop's details.** On the chart, press a stop's name in "The day": the focus moves to "About <place>"; Tab reaches Close and the map link; Escape folds it and returns to the name. With the details open, Tab on until the focus is on something they cover; Escape folds them, and the focus stays.
- **The day's menu.** "Take this day" opens a menu; the arrows move, Escape returns to the button, and "Copy the link" says "The day's link is copied".
- **An ending without a day.** With live generation on and a day that fails, the reason is said once as the page turns, and the focus lands on the city word.
- **A re-plan.** With live generation on, change "early dinner" to "eating late": while it is written a status says so once; whole, the answers "eating late Take, proposed" and "early dinner, your day" are a radio group reached by Tab, the arrows choose, and the choice is said; another word pressed meanwhile says "one change at a time".
- **The theme.** "Switch to dark theme" says "The page is dark" once, and the focus stays on the button, now named "Switch to light theme".
- **NVDA alone, browse mode over a stop on the plan.** Arrow down onto a stop: browse mode reads the stop's words, Tab or Enter enters it, and the arrow keys swap versions only in focus mode. Known at risk: whether the hint's arrow keys hold in browse mode.
- **NVDA alone, two lists.** Open the energy list, then Tab away: the list closes, and no two lists stand open.
- **NVDA alone, the city list.** Open the city word: a group "Where" with its rows and a field "Somewhere else"; the arrows move through the rows and the field; Escape returns to the word.
- **NVDA alone, an unseen day's play.** Open `/day/kanazawa` in a private window and read in browse mode while it plays: "Writing the day" is said once, rows not yet written are not read, and nothing more is said when the day is whole. Open it again in a new private window and press "Show the whole day" under the day while it plays: the city word is said, the whole day reads from the headline down, and no status says the day is still being written.

### Voice control

With Voice Control on macOS or iOS: say "click calm" with the energy list open, then "click quiet"; say "click Take" on a proposal; name the start screen's field. Known at risk: the field's name holds none of the words shown around it.

### Keyboard alone

In Chrome, and in Safari with "Press Tab to highlight each item" on: Tab through `/`, a day in its plan and its chart, a proposal, `/privacy` and `/day/vienna`. Every control is reached in reading order, the 2 px ring shows on each, nothing traps the focus, and nothing focused is wholly hidden by the plan's bar or the phone's energy scale. While an unseen day plays, Tab reaches "Show the whole day" under the day, and Enter lands the day whole with the focus on the city word. Known: on the chart, a changed part's answers come after the stops they stand over. Make a day, change its energy, swap a stop and take it along through the menu by keys alone: Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, the arrows and Escape.

### Zoom and text size

- Browser zoom at 200% and 400% at 1280 px wide, in Chrome and Safari: no sideways scroll outside the chart's drawing, nothing clipped, and the masthead and the plan's bar do not cover the focus.
- Text size at 200% (Chrome's font size "Very large", Safari's Cmd Option +): text grows and wraps. Known: the chart's hour labels clip.
- macOS Zoom following the keyboard's focus round the chart and a stop's details: the focused element stays in the zoomed view.

### Reduced motion

Turn on Reduce motion (macOS) or turn off animation effects (Windows), open an unseen day, swap, change the energy and the theme. Nothing slides, leans or fades between themes, rows land whole once written, and the theme changes at once. The unseen day's play still lasts about ten seconds, unless "Show the whole day" under the day is pressed, which lands it whole at once. A person sensitive to motion, with no system setting, watches an unseen day and the start screen's wait, and notes the play's length and the lantern's light.

### Forced colours and colour vision

- Windows contrast themes (Aquatic, Desert, Dusk, Night sky): every word, control and ring shows, and the chart's curve and beads are drawn (`docs/decisions.md`, "Under forced colours the system's theme stands").
- With a person or a simulator for deuteranopia and protanopia, read a day's energy, the assumed words and a proposal: nothing is told by colour alone.

### Touch

Swipe a stop on the plan, tap its arrows, and tap the beads on the chart: every swap can be made by one tap. With VoiceOver on iOS, swipe through `/day/kanazawa`, double-tap "Livelier: …" and open "Take this day"; the plan's bar can be reached and its waiting state is said. With TalkBack on Android, the same, and "Copy the link".

## Keeping this document
