# Targeted re-plan: the contract (proposed 2026-10-31)

**Status: accepted on 2026-10-31, with the answers below; the schema, the prompt, the windows, the merge, the reader and `POST /api/replan` built ("Built so far"), with `replan-windows`, the golden set of six and the frames the view reads. The view is the fork: the new part is proposed beside the day, and the visitor takes it (`docs/decisions.md`, "A changed word proposes its part beside the day, and the visitor takes it").**

## The problem

Before this contract, a changed word other than the energy re-planned the whole day, as a word no stop names still does (`docs/decisions.md`, "A changed word proposes its part beside the day, and the visitor takes it"). The owner changed "slow start" to "early start" and watched every stop be written again, when only the morning should have changed. Each stop already says which words shaped it: `dependsOn` in `GeneratedStopSchema`, described to the model as "an answer that changes one of them plans this stop again". The contract below rewrites only the part of the day a changed word shaped, and keeps the rest exactly as the visitor has it, their swaps included.

The figures come from the last three day rounds (`day-v4`, `day-v5`, `day-v6`): 36 Sonnet days, 293 stops, of which 22 (8%) name no word in `dependsOn`.

## 1. Which words touch which stops

| changed word | what is re-planned                                                                                                 | why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `start`      | one window from the first stop through the last stop that names `start`                                            | Sonnet names `start` in every day. In 28 of 36 it is one run from the first stop; in the other 8 it also names a later stop, most often lunch (09:45\* … 13:30\*), so the window takes the morning through it. Median 25% of the stops  |
| `end`        | one window from the first stop that names `end` through the last stop                                              | One run to the last stop in 24 of 35 days. As a suffix: median 14% of the stops, never more than half                                                                                                                                   |
| `dinner`     | each run of stops that name `dinner`, as its own window                                                            | One run in 30 of 36 days, two in 6; median 14% of the stops, never more than half                                                                                                                                                       |
| `travel`     | the word alone, no call (since 2026-11-08)                                                                         | A day is planned a walk's way across whatever the travel word, so travel's re-plans only shuffled the same districts' places (`docs/harness.md`, "Every word's windows"). A travel word that widens the day is a roadmap candidate      |
| `company`    | each run of stops that name `company`, as its own window; the word alone where no stop names it (since 2026-11-08) | Up to four windows, over half the day in 11 of 36, none at all in 15. On the written days, Bologna and Nafplio re-plan part of the day, Madrid's six stops of seven the whole of it, and Porto, Kanazawa and Utrecht name it on no stop |
| `energy`     | nothing: the curve re-picks among the versions already written, with no call                                       | Unchanged (`docs/decisions.md`, "The energy curve stays on the client and picks among the versions the model wrote")                                                                                                                    |

Two fallbacks make a targeted re-plan a full one:

- **No stop names a time word.** The model's own map says nothing depends on it, yet the visitor changed it, and its hours are a promise; a full re-plan is the safe reading. Company no stop names changes the word alone, and so does travel (`docs/decisions.md`, "A changed word proposes its part beside the day, and the visitor takes it").
- **The windows hold more than two thirds of the stops.** Then little is kept and the seams cost more than they save. For `start` this happens in 3 of 36 recorded days.

The windows for `start` and `end` lean on the model's tags only for where they stop, not for which stops lie inside, so a stop it forgot to tag inside the morning is re-planned with it.

**A window also takes the kept stops the new word leaves no room for** (built 2026-10-31, after the first build failed two of the golden cases on paper), since the merge never moves a kept stop. A slow start takes every stop that begins before 10:00, the first hour of a slow morning (amended 2026-11-05, below): the written Kyoto day tags only its 07:30 temple, and its 08:25 walk left a slow morning nowhere to go. A late dinner takes the stops after it that start before 20:30: the recorded Lisbon day's 19:00 walk after its 17:30 dinner. An early dinner takes the stops before it that end after 19:30, an evening end, home by eight, those that end after 19:00, the hour its last stop needs before eight (2026-11-07), and an early end, home by eleven, those that end after 22:00, the hour its last stop needs before eleven (2026-11-11): taking only those that ended after 23:00 left a day whose kept dinner ended at eleven no room at all. The margins are `time-words`' own, now in the schema beside `EARLY_START_BY`.

## 2. What goes to the model

The same model as the day (Sonnet 5.5) and a new prompt, `replan-v1`. It shares the day prompt's plan and voice rules (`src/lib/live/day-prompt.ts`) and adds a section on re-planning. Three things arrive as data, each in its own fenced block, as the request does today:

1. **The request with the changed word**: the same city, zone and date as the day the server reads (since 2026-11-02, below, question 1), so the city is the one checked when that day was written. **Since `replan-v7` (2026-11-05)** each other word comes as the kept day has it, chosen by the visitor or assumed by Lantern, read from the facts the merged day's headline shows (`replannedFactsOf` in `src/lib/replan/merge.ts`), and a word is open only when Lantern asked about it. Until then the request came as `dayPromptFor` writes it for a new day, every word left to Lantern open, so the model read what Lantern had assumed for the kept day as free: production's Elche day assumed "home by eleven", and its dinner re-planned to eating late ran to 23:45 (`docs/decisions.md`, "A changed word proposes its part beside the day, and the visitor takes it").
2. **The kept stops**, in time order. For each, the version on screen (its `selectedPlaceId`, so a swap the visitor made is what the new stops fit around; since 2026-11-02 the server reads its own record of the day (question 1), and since 2026-11-07 the page sends the visitor's picks by id for the server to plan the day on, `docs/decisions.md`, "A re-plan reads its day from the server's own records"): start time, minutes, name, kind, neighbourhood, coordinates and energy. No lines: the model needs to fit around a stop, not to write it. Only these places are named, and the rules ask never to repeat one. `replan-v7` (2026-11-05) also listed every place the kept stops hold, their options included (`keptPlaceNamesOf`), which is what the merge reads a repeat against; read as places to go, the options drew a new stop in four of its eight live re-plans of the golden four, each then not shown, and `replan-v8` (2026-11-06) lists them no more (`docs/decisions.md`, "A changed word proposes its part beside the day, and the visitor takes it").
3. **The windows.** Each says when it opens and closes, and which kept stops stand either side of it, so the walk to and from them is planned. A window at the day's start opens where the start word says the day begins; a window at the day's end closes where the end word says it ends. A window's old stops come with it, by name and kind only, and the prompt allows keeping one when it still suits the new word: an earlier start may well keep the temple, at dawn.

The model writes **only the new stops, their alternatives, and a new line about the day**:

- The output schema is the day's minus `assumptions`: `stops` (`GeneratedStopSchema`), `alternatives` (`GeneratedAlternativeSetSchema`, its `stopIndex` into the new stops) and `dek`, in that order. The new stops stream first because they are what changes on screen, and the line is written after the stops it names.
- It keeps the `Generated*` rules: strict objects, every field required, no numeric, length, count, pattern or format keywords, which the schema test enforces. It has fewer union types than the day's, since the five nullable assumptions are gone. The schema probe (`EVAL_ROUND=schema pnpm eval:live`) runs before any live round.
- An output cap of 6,000 tokens, half the day's 12,000: a day of about eight stops runs to about 6,500 tokens, and a window holds two or three stops, rarely six.
- Which window a new stop belongs to is read from its time, so the schema carries no window index.

## 3. How the answer merges with the day

A pure function in `src/lib` merges the day on screen with the re-plan. It must load in a browser, as `src/lib/server-timing.ts` does, because the stream reader normalizes on both sides, and the view is built on it.

- **Kept stops are untouched:** `id`, `startTime`, `durationMinutes`, `primary`, `alternatives`, `selectedPlaceId` and `dependsOn`, byte for byte.
- **New stops are normalized as a day's stops are**, with the normalizer's repairs, and take ids under the re-plan's own prefix: `r2-stop-0`, `r2-stop-0:p`, `r2-stop-0:a1`. The number is the page's count of re-plans, sent by the client and bounded by the request schema. Place ids double as Motion `layoutId`s, so a second re-plan reusing `stop-0:p` for a different place would morph one place into another; a prefix makes every id the page has shown unique. One exception keeps a place's id: a new stop whose place is spelled as a window stop's, the place its row shows, its pick where the visitor made one, at that stop's start, stands for it (`stayingStopOf`, 2026-11-06), and keeps the old stop's id and its place as the day had it, point and check included; its length, versions and dependencies are the re-plan's, its versions under the prefix. The same place under the same id morphs nothing, and its row stays where it stood rather than one leaving and one landing.
- **A new stop's `selectedPlaceId` is its primary.** The curve's local re-pick for the energy word then applies to the merged day as to any day.
- **Order is by start time.** Kept and new stops are sorted together. A new stop starts after the kept stop before it ends and ends before the kept stop after it starts.
- **The line about the day is replaced** by the re-plan's, since it names the day's places in order.
- **The facts:** the changed word becomes `chosen` with its new value; every other word stays as it was.
- **The new stops' `dependsOn`** is kept as written, with the energy word added, as for a day.

## 4. When the model writes what it must not

The model never sees a kept stop in its output schema, so it cannot change one: the contract makes that edit impossible to write. What it can still get wrong, and what the merge does about each:

| fault                                                                            | what happens                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A new stop outside every window                                                  | Dropped (`outside-windows`), and so is the whole re-plan (below)                                                                                     |
| A new stop that overlaps a kept stop                                             | Shortened to fit (`shortened-at-kept`); dropped if under 15 minutes remain (`dropped-at-kept`), and so is the whole re-plan. A kept stop never moves |
| A new stop that repeats a kept stop's place, by name as the place match reads it | Dropped (a new repair, `dropped-repeat`), and so is the whole re-plan                                                                                |
| An alternative set that points at no new stop                                    | Dropped, as the day's normalizer does (`dropped-alternatives`)                                                                                       |
| A new stop's option past the walk, blank, or at another new stop                 | Dropped, as the day's normalizer does (`dropped-alternative`, `dropped-repeat`); its stop stands, and so does the re-plan                            |
| **A window left with no stop**                                                   | **The whole re-plan fails**, and the day stays as it was                                                                                             |
| A stream cut before its end (the token cap, an error)                            | The whole re-plan fails, and the day stays as it was                                                                                                 |

A re-plan is all or nothing. **Since 2026-11-05 a dropped new stop fails it too** (`mergeShownReplan`, which the page's reader and the server's both read the outcome through; `docs/decisions.md`, "A changed word proposes its part beside the day, and the visitor takes it"): the line about the day is written after the stops and names them, so once one is dropped it names a stop the day does not have, and the hours it held stand empty. Production's Elche evening came back to the morning's coffee square; the merge dropped the stop, and the day showed "coffee on the square again" with nothing from 17:30 to 21:00. A stop cut short at a kept one still stands. The route makes one call; a retry, if one is wanted, is the page's. A failed one takes the path a failed full re-plan takes: the day stays exactly as it was, and its fork row says why (`replanEndedOf` in `src/features/day/not-yet.ts`). An empty window does not keep its old stops: they were planned under the old word, and a morning that silently still starts late would claim a word the day does not keep. As for an empty day today, a failed re-plan gives the visitor's count back.

## 5. How it differs from a full re-plan in the log and the limits

| The visitor's count  | one of their ten days                            | one of their ten days (recommended; see the questions below)                                                                                                                        |
| The app's spend      | the day's worst case and the shadow's held       | a smaller worst case: the input ceiling and the 6,000-token cap; no shadow                                                                                                          |
| The shadow on Haiku  | yes                                              | no, until a golden set of re-plans exists: the shadow is the eval's instrument, and this has no eval yet                                                                            |
| `generation_log`     | `role` day and shadow, `prompt_version` `day-v6` | `role` day, `prompt_version` `replan-v1`, which already tells it apart; `request` is the new request, `output_text` the re-plan as written                                          |
| Replaying it offline | the request reproduces the call                  | needs the changed word, the windows and the kept stops: a new nullable `context` column (one small migration), so a re-plan can be scored again and re-run like a day (recommended) |

## 6. Quality checks

On the merged day, **every check a day runs**: shape, normalizes, asked words, banned phrases, meal name, script, escaped letters, alternatives map, swap direction, repairs, walk radius, energy fit, day flow and light sense. The seams between kept and new stops count toward the day flow and the legs like any others.

On the re-plan as written, before its repairs, **one new hard check, `replan-windows`**: each finding names a new stop outside its window, one overlapping a kept stop, one repeating a kept place, or a window left empty. The merge repairs the first three, but the eval has to see how often the model needed it, as `repairs` counts for a day.

On the day the re-plan merged into, a dropped stop and all, so the record shows the day it would have made, **four more hard checks** (`src/lib/quality/replan-checks.ts`), each recorded on every re-plan's row beside `replan-windows`: `replan-word`, the changed word kept (2026-10-31, `docs/harness.md`); and since 2026-11-05, `replan-time-words`, every word that sets the day's hours kept, start, dinner and end, the ones Lantern assumed as well as the visitor's; `replan-drops`, how many new stops the merge left out and why, its `value` the count; and `replan-gap`, a new stretch of more than two hours between stops that the kept day had nowhere near it. Production's Elche evening fails all three: its end, assumed early, ends at 23:45; the coffee is dropped; and 17:30 to 21:00 stands empty.

On every day, not only re-plans, **one new check, `time-words`**: the day keeps its start, dinner and end words, by the definitions the schema already gives the model. Nothing checked this, for a first day or a full re-plan, though it is the promise a changed time word makes. **Built 2026-10-31** (`docs/harness.md`, "The start, dinner and end words"), with its margins set on the recorded days: an early start is a first stop by 08:30, since no recorded day began at first light; a slow one from 09:00 to 11:30; dinner, the first meal from 17:00, by 19:30 or from 20:30; the last stop ending by 23:00 or at midnight and after. A re-plan reads its changed word's finding there.

**A golden set of four re-plans, as fixtures from recorded days**, each with its windows written by hand: a `start` whose tag is one run (the case that started this contract, slow to early), a `start` that also names lunch, a `dinner` from early to late, and an `end` from early to late. The merge and `replan-windows` are tested on them with no call. The live round, once its worst case is confirmed, is the schema probe and then these four, and two more, on Sonnet: a re-plan writes a third or less of a day's tokens, so about $0.03 to $0.05 each, under $0.50 in all.

| piece                                                                 | hours        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |

| The re-plan schema, `replan-v1` and their tests                       | 3            |
| Windows and the merge, pure, with the golden fixtures                 | 3 to 4       |
| `POST /api/replan`: signature, limits, log, stream, tests on the fake | 3            |
| The reader for a re-plan's stream, giving merged partial days         | 2 to 3       |
| The `context` column                                                  | 1            |
| **In all, before the view**                                           | **14 to 17** |

**Payback.** A changed time word writes two or three stops instead of eight or nine: the visitor waits for those alone, a re-plan costs a third of a day or less, and the rest of the day, its swaps included, never moves under them.

**What it displaces.** These weeks also hold MVP's place grounding (outcome 3: the route verifies no place yet) and the eval script and its README table (outcome 5, second in the cut order). `time-words` serves the eval as well as this, so it goes first; the contract after it.

- **The frames**, `replanFrameOf` in `src/features/abacus/replan-frame.ts`. The frames as first built, by the view chosen on 2026-11-01, "Struck through, one in, one out": from the basis and each partial re-plan, the rows in time order, each with its `source`: `"kept"` for a stop outside every window, the same `Stop` object; `"old"` for a window's old stop still on screen, struck through; `"new"` for a new stop as a `StopDraft` under `r{round}-stop-N`, `isWhole` once the stream has moved past it, and `hasVersions` once its set of versions is whole, which it carries only then; `"stays"` (2026-11-06) for a whole new stop at a window stop's place and start, under that stop's id and with its place, in its row. A window's old stops that no new stop stands for leave one for each new stop that lands in it, the earliest first, and the rest on `complete`; `leaving` lists the old rows' ids, and `idPrefix` the round's prefix, which says the stops it added (`newStopsOf`). A whole new stop is shown as the normalizer orders it and the merge places it: dropped when it starts no later than the new stop before it, outside every window or on a kept place's name; cut at the next new stop's start when it runs into it; cut at the next kept stop when it runs into that, and dropped when that leaves it under 15 minutes. So a whole stop keeps its time and length when the merged day takes over, and only one the normalizer cannot show, a blank line or a point off the map, still goes then; the tests hold the rows to the merged day's ids, times and lengths. `windows` gives each window's `opens` and `closes` in minutes of the day, null at the day's start or end; `dek` the re-plan's line as far as written. `dayAfterReplan` is the merged day on `complete` and the day as it was on any other ending. Tested on a window of two old stops and three new, of three old and one new, a stream cut inside the window, two windows, and the merge's and the normalizer's cuts and drops (`replan-frame.test.ts`). `source` gained `"old"` over the agreed `"kept"` or `"new"`: the old stops must be rows for the view to strike them in time order.
- **The live round**, `EVAL_ROUND=replan pnpm eval:live` (`src/eval/replan-round.ts`): the golden set, or another set `EVAL_REPLAN_SET` names (`scripts/live-eval.ts`), through the re-plan route's own code, on the day's model with no shadow and no place checked, each read as the page reads it, its partials made frames by `replanFrameOf`. ## Questions settled before the build

**Answered on 2026-10-31:** what the model reads of the kept stops, A, bounded data in a fence, with hard limits on the server; one count, a re-plan one of the visitor's ten days; no shadow; and the `context` column, applied to production by hand.

**Question 1 changed on 2026-11-02 to C,** with the security fix (`docs/security.md`, S1; `docs/decisions.md`, "A re-plan reads its day from the server's own records"). Under A, the day kept under a re-plan's id was rebuilt from the client's day, so its link showed any words the client wrote, on Lantern's domain. The page now names the day, a written day by its address or a kept one by its id, and sends the request of enums, the word and the count; the server reads the day, writes its own copy into `context`, and shows only a re-plan whose `context` names that basis. C's race, a word changed before the day's record lands, is read again for about two seconds, then sent on to the whole day with a 409.

1. **What the model reads of the kept stops, and whether it can trust it.** Today the city is the only free text from the client that the model reads, and it is signed for that reason. Kept stops sent back by the client would be a second channel. Three ways, cheapest first:
   - **A. Bounded data in a fence** (recommended for now). Names, kinds, neighbourhoods, times, coordinates and energy only, each bounded in length, in a fenced block the prompt calls data, as the request is. It protects the shape and the size. It does not stop someone from writing an instruction into a name, which spends their own day, under their own count and the app's cap, and still comes back in the re-plan's schema. About an hour.
   - **B. The server signs the day it wrote.** The stream ends with a signature of the day as written; the client sends the day and the signature back, and a re-plan signs the merged day it returns. The kept stops are then exactly what the server wrote. A change to the stream and the reader the view builds on, 4 to 6 hours.
   - **C. The server looks the day up in `generation_log`** by an id the day's answer names. As safe as B, but it adds a database read before the call, and the record lands in `after()`, once the stream has ended, so a change made a second or two later can find nothing. A re-plan's record must also hold the merged day for the next re-plan. 4 to 6 hours.
2. **One count or two.** A re-plan as one of the visitor's ten days is simplest, and is how a full re-plan counts today. A separate allowance for re-plans would reflect that one costs a third of a day, at the price of a third limit to explain.
3. **No shadow for re-plans** until their golden set exists (recommended above).
